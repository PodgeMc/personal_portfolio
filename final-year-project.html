Temporal Thief

A 3D stealth puzzle game built around time loops, echoes, and paradoxes.

Temporal Thief is my final year university project and an ongoing personal game project. Built in Unity, it explores how recorded player actions can become both puzzle solutions and gameplay threats across multiple runs.

Overview

Temporal Thief is a 3D stealth puzzle game where the player must move through a level, collect an objective item, and escape without being detected. The key idea behind the game is that each run is recorded and replayed in later attempts as a ghost-like echo of the player’s earlier actions.

These echoes can help solve puzzles by repeating useful actions, but they can also become a danger. If the player is seen by one of their past selves, a paradox is triggered and the level resets.

Why It Stands Out

Time as the Core Mechanic

Time manipulation is not a side feature here. It is the foundation of the gameplay.

Past Actions Matter

Every run changes the level because your previous movements return as active echoes.

Stealth + Puzzle Design

The project combines stealth tension with puzzle solving that unfolds across multiple runs.

Player Becomes the Obstacle

Your own earlier choices can block paths, create risk, or force smarter planning later.

Core Mechanics

1. Echo Recording

During each run, the game records player movement, rotation, and timing data. This creates a timeline of what the player did, which can then be reused in the next loop.

Technical Implementation

  • Unity game engine
  • C# scripting
  • Recording and playback systems for player timelines
  • Interpolation for smoother echo movement
  • Raycasting for line-of-sight paradox detection
  • Loop management for spawning, saving runs, and restarting attempts
  • Animation logic tied to movement speed for consistency between player and echoes

Challenges & Solutions

  • Making playback feel smooth and predictable instead of stiff or jittery.
  • Ensuring echoes and player spawns always appeared in the correct positions.
  • Making paradox detection feel fair rather than frustrating.
  • Keeping animation behaviour consistent between live gameplay and replayed echoes.
  • Balancing an interesting mechanic with a realistic final year project scope.
As development continued, predefined spawn points improved consistency, a short delay helped paradox resets feel fairer, and animation logic was adjusted so echoes looked more natural.
